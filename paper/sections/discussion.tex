\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What transfers along the memory hierarchy}

The primitive transfers. The flatness score chooses a kd-tree or a quadtree once per scene, and that choice holds from one million to one billion points, from the workstation to the server, and from memory to every out-of-core placement measured: the best structure with the leaves on a device has the primitive of the in-memory pick on every cloud. The leaf does not transfer. It follows the device, from 64 to 128 points in memory to 512 or 2\,048 on a solid-state drive and 8\,192 on a hard disk, and it follows the page size, since the huge-page gain is largest for the variants with the most nodes. A rule for practitioners therefore has two parts: a primitive from the scene's geometry, and a leaf from the device's latency--bandwidth product and the page size.

The second transferable lesson is about measurement. In memory, node counts do not rank structures, because a visit near the root costs a tenth of one near a leaf; out of core, reads and bytes do rank them, because there a read is the cost. A study that reports counts in memory, or times on one device and generalises to another, reports a different quantity from the one a user pays.

\subsection{Recommendations}

\paragraph{In memory.} For a three-dimensional scene (terrestrial or mobile scans, forests, heritage), a sliding-midpoint kd-tree with a leaf of 64 to 128 points, as deep as the data takes it; nanoflann's default is already this structure. For a 2.5-dimensional scene (aerial terrain and cities), a quadtree splitting at the points' centre with a leaf of 64 to 128. Enable transparent huge pages for any index whose node pool exceeds a few tens of megabytes: the gain is \hugeBigPools$\times$ for pools over 64\,MB here, and a server in \emph{always} mode gets it for free; on huge pages a kd-tree's best leaf moves down to 16 to 32 points. Do not prefetch the children of wide nodes.

\paragraph{Out of core.} Keep the hierarchy in memory: at 100M points it is a few tens of megabytes, and paging it costs several times more than any choice of structure saves. Grow the leaf until scanning it costs about as much as a read, and keep the primitive chosen for the scene.

\paragraph{For measuring.} Pin the process, rotate the order, take medians, and time structures alone or say that they were interleaved: co-resident indexes slow a memory-bound tree by up to a fifth through translation reach alone. For library authors, the gap between implementations of the same kd-tree is 2.6$\times$ (Section~\ref{sec:libraries}), larger than any lever in this paper except the primitive; an octree at leaf 32 is a poor default for dense scans, and an index that reorders its points into tree order wins the radius queries.

\paragraph{On nesting.} Nested variants were searched on equal terms with single structures and never beat a tuned one with the hierarchy in memory (Appendix~\ref{sec:composition}). They save visits near the root, which are the cheap ones, and help only when the hierarchy itself is paged from a slow device without a cache, a placement that keeping a few tens of megabytes in memory avoids.

\subsection{Threats to validity}

Clouds of 100 million points and more, the libraries and the layout experiments ran on one server, shared with other users on other cores; the smaller clouds ran on one workstation; no ratio crosses the machines, but the absolute latencies are those of two processors and four devices. Reads from a device are synchronous at queue depth one, and the cache has one replacement policy. The query traces are the queries three pipeline stages issue, derived from the cloud's spacing rather than recorded inside a third-party pipeline, and within a scene density co-varies with size, since the size tiers are subsets of one scan. The genetic search is one search method; a budget-matched random search matched it in an earlier battery, but the 55-structure grid bounds what any rule can pick. The rule was fitted on sixteen scenes. The external libraries were built from conda-forge without AVX for the two whose search code is precompiled, which may overstate their gap by some percent. Finally, two compiler effects would have invalidated the layout experiments had they gone unnoticed: the compiler inlined the traversal templates into their entry points and slowed the default path by up to fifteen percent, and it deleted the prefetch variant entirely because a routine that only prefetches has no visible effect. Both were caught by an A/B test against the previous binary and by reading the disassembly; a prefetch experiment must be verified in the binary, not in the source.
